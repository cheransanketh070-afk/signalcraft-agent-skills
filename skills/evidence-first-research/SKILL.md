# Evidence-First Research

Turn a broad question into a decision-ready brief without laundering uncertainty into confident prose.

## Use this when
- The answer depends on current facts, laws, prices, software versions, public figures, market conditions, or niche claims.
- A user needs a sourced explainer, comparison, due-diligence note, or research memo.

## Inputs
Identify the question, audience, geography, date range, intended decision, and required depth. If one missing detail materially changes the result, state a reasonable assumption and proceed unless the task truly cannot be answered.

## Workflow
1. **Scope the question.** Split it into answerable claims. Define what evidence would change the recommendation.
2. **Search for primary evidence first.** Prefer official docs, original datasets, filings, research papers, standards, and direct statements. Use credible secondary sources for context, independent verification, or where primary evidence is unavailable.
3. **Check freshness and provenance.** Record publication date and event date separately when they differ. Open the actual source, not only the search snippet. Prefer the latest applicable version for time-sensitive topics.
4. **Triangulate important claims.** For consequential claims, look for independent corroboration. Multiple pages repeating one press release do not count as independent confirmation.
5. **Keep an evidence ledger.** For each material claim, note the claim, source URL/title, source date, evidence excerpt or paraphrase, reliability, and confidence. Distinguish sourced fact, inference, estimate, and opinion.
6. **Resolve contradictions visibly.** Explain whether the disagreement comes from different dates, definitions, methods, populations, or truly conflicting evidence. Do not silently choose the convenient source.
7. **Write to the decision.** Lead with the finding, then implications, evidence, limitations, and the next check. Cite claims near the sentence they support.
8. **Run a fabrication check.** Verify every citation exists and supports the nearby claim. Never invent URLs, quotations, dates, statistics, studies, or source names.

## Output format
- **Bottom line** — 2–4 sentences.
- **What the evidence says** — concise findings with inline source links.
- **Evidence ledger** — claim / source / date / confidence.
- **What remains uncertain** — missing or conflicting evidence.
- **Practical next step** — one action that reduces the most important uncertainty.

## Rules
- If live browsing or source access is unavailable, say so and provide a clearly labeled background-only answer or research plan.
- Do not present the absence of evidence as evidence of absence.
- For high-stakes topics, flag jurisdiction, date, and professional review requirements.
- Summarize sources in original language; do not reproduce long copyrighted passages.

## Example trigger
“Research the current options for hosting a small Python app. Compare price, sleep behavior, limits, and official documentation. Cite every current claim and say what you could not verify.”
