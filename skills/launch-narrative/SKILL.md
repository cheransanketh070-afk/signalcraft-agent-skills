# Launch Narrative

Make a launch understandable and compelling by connecting a specific audience, painful problem, credible promise, and visible proof.

## Workflow
1. **Collect the facts.** Product, audience, current alternative, top use case, constraints, price, availability, differentiator, and actual proof. Label assumptions; do not treat aspirations as traction.
2. **Pick one primary user.** Define who has the problem now, how they handle it today, why the workaround is costly, and what event makes them seek a solution.
3. **Write positioning.** Use: “For [specific audience] who [problem], [product] helps [outcome] by [mechanism], unlike [alternative] because [verifiable difference].” Refine for clarity, not buzzword density.
4. **Build a proof ladder.** Prefer live demo, reproducible example, screenshots, documented performance, user quote with permission, or public benchmark. If no proof exists, show a transparent demo or mark the claim as a hypothesis.
5. **Create the core narrative.** Problem → cost of status quo → insight → how it works → example → limitations → next action. Include what the product does not do when this prevents misleading expectations.
6. **Adapt deliverables.** Draft a concise README/landing-page hero, three benefit bullets, use-case example, FAQ, CTA, and channel-native launch posts. Each channel should add a reason to care rather than paste the same paragraph everywhere.
7. **Prepare objections.** Answer setup time, price, privacy, compatibility, reliability, support, switching cost, and who should not use it.
8. **Define a small learning loop.** Track qualified visits, activation, completion, conversion, and user feedback. Avoid vanity metrics alone and disclose the baseline window.
9. **Run a claims audit.** Verify every number, quote, testimonial, comparison, availability statement, and superlative. Never invent customers, stars, downloads, revenue, case studies, or urgency.

## Output package
- One-sentence positioning
- Landing hero: headline, subheadline, primary CTA
- Three proof-backed benefits
- One short demo/storyboard
- FAQ with honest limitations
- Launch post variants for requested channels
- Measurement plan with event definitions

## Example trigger
“Help me launch a free open-source developer tool. I have a demo but no users yet. Give me a strong message without pretending I have traction.”
