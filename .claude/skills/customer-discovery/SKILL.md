# Customer Discovery

Learn whether a problem is real, frequent, and costly before building a feature or spending money on promotion.

## Workflow
1. **State the hypothesis.** Define a specific audience, job to be done, triggering situation, current workaround, and assumed pain. Write what evidence would disprove the idea.
2. **Find an appropriate sample.** Use public communities, opt-in lists, professional networks, existing users, and referrals in ways their rules permit. Do not scrape private groups, collect sensitive personal data, or automate unwanted mass messages.
3. **Recruit transparently.** Say who you are, why you are reaching out, how long it takes, whether anything is offered, and how information will be used. Make declining easy; do not disguise a pitch as research.
4. **Ask about past behavior.** Start with “Tell me about the last time…” and “What did you do next?” Ask what they tried, what it cost, what failed, and how they choose alternatives. Avoid “Would you use a tool that…?” as primary evidence.
5. **Avoid leading questions.** Ask one question at a time. Do not praise a preferred answer, imply that a problem should exist, or reveal the proposed solution before understanding the current workflow.
6. **Capture evidence carefully.** Separate direct quote, observed behavior, interpretation, and assumption. Get permission before recording. Minimize identifiers and use anonymized notes where possible.
7. **Look for disconfirming evidence.** Interview non-users or people who chose another option. Distinguish an inconvenient problem from a high-priority, budgeted problem.
8. **Synthesize patterns.** Group by triggering event, frequency, workaround, cost, decision process, alternatives, and urgency. Count how many people show a pattern and describe the sample limitations.
9. **Run a low-cost test.** Choose the smallest ethical test: manual concierge, waitlist with transparent wording, prototype task, or small opt-in offer. Define success and failure thresholds before running it.
10. **Decide.** Continue, narrow, change the segment, or stop. Do not infer market size from a handful of convenience interviews.

## Deliverables
- Hypothesis and falsification criteria
- Recruiting message
- 6–10 non-leading interview questions
- Synthesis matrix
- Evidence versus assumptions
- Next experiment and a predefined threshold
